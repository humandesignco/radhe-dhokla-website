Surat, 1995. One steamer, one family, and a sprinkle of tadka that people still cross the city for.

1995 · The founder

Kanaiyalal Pandav
and one steamer.

Radhe Dhokla began as a small family business in a parking area in Surat. Kanaiyalal Pandav sold one thing — dhokla — and did it with a care that regulars noticed immediately: the batter was fermented overnight, the trays were steamed in small batches, and every plate was finished with his signature tempering of mustard seeds, curry leaves and chilli.

The name came from his devotion. Radhe. And the tagline that still sits under the logo — સ્વાદ સાથે સંબંધ… — says what the stall was really selling: a relationship, with taste.

2015 · The second generation

Jinesh and Priyanka
join the counter.

Twenty years in, the founder's son Jinesh Pandav and daughter Priyanka Pandav joined the business with new ideas. The same year, Radhe added Indian sabzis, Chinese and pav bhaji — and made one decision that changed everything: sell it by the kilogram.

A parcel became a full dinner for a family. A kilo of khaman became the easiest thing to bring to a society function. The counter stopped being a snack stop and became the way a city fed its guests.

The vision

રસોડું અમારું, સાંજ તમારી.The kitchen is ours. The evening is yours.

In most homes, one person — usually a woman — spends the best hours of a celebration standing at a stove. Radhe exists so that she can sit at the table, laugh with her guests, and still serve food that tastes like it was made at home. Delegate the cooking to Radhe. Keep the time for the people.
